## Research Findings

CRISPR gene editing has profoundly revolutionized modern agriculture by shifting crop breeding from a decades-long endeavor into a process achievable within just a few growing seasons. Through foundational technologies and advanced iterations such as base and prime editing, scientists can now execute precise single-nucleotide modifications without inducing double-strand DNA breaks, thereby minimizing off-target effects and maximizing accuracy. These capabilities have unlocked unprecedented potential for addressing severe environmental challenges, facilitating multiplex gene editing that simultaneously targets quantitative trait loci for drought tolerance, heat stress resilience, and broad-spectrum pathogen resistance across vital staple and orphan crops.

Beyond agronomic resilience, CRISPR applications have driven significant advancements in global food security and nutritional enhancement. Metabolic pathway engineering, facilitated by systems like CRISPR-Cas12a, has successfully upregulated key nutrients such as provitamin A and essential amino acids in vulnerable crops while simultaneously reducing the agricultural sector's reliance on chemical pesticides. This dual focus on nutritional quality and environmental sustainability positions gene editing as a cornerstone of future agricultural productivity.
